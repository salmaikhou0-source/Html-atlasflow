How to answer French customer emails without hiring

Updated November 27, 2026 · 6-minute read

If you sell across Canada, some of your customers will write to you in French. About one in five Canadians lives in Quebec, and there are French speakers in every province, including about 650,000 in Ontario. When those emails arrive and nobody on your team writes French well, they tend to sit unanswered or get a reply pasted from a translation app.

Here are the three realistic options, what each one costs, and five replies in Canadian French you can use today.

Option 1: Use a translation app

Free and fast. It's fine for understanding what a customer wrote. For replying, it's risky: machine translations often sound stiff, get the tone wrong, and use France French words that customers in Quebec notice right away. A customer who wrote politely in French and gets an awkward reply often feels the company doesn't care about them.

Best for: a handful of French messages a month, read internally.

Option 2: Hire a bilingual employee

The most control, and the most cost. A full-time customer service employee in Toronto costs about $4,000 or more a month once you add payroll costs and vacation pay, before recruiting and training. People who write well in both languages are also hard to find, and when your only French speaker is sick, on vacation or leaves, French support stops.

Best for: companies with hundreds of French messages every week.

Option 3: Outsource only your French messages

Your team keeps handling English. A bilingual team answers the French messages from inside your own inbox, under your company's name. You pay a monthly fee instead of a salary, and coverage for sick days and vacations is the provider's problem.

Best for: most small and mid-sized businesses that get some French messages every week. This is what we do at Bilingual Support Co.: our French Desk plan starts at $250 a month, with a free 2-week trial.

5 replies in Canadian French you can copy

Replace the parts in [brackets]. Each reply uses vous, the polite form customers expect from a business.

1. Where is my order?

Bonjour [Prénom],

Merci de nous avoir écrit. Votre commande n° [numéro] a été expédiée le [date] et devrait arriver d'ici le [date]. Vous pouvez suivre votre colis ici : [lien].

N'hésitez pas à nous écrire si vous avez d'autres questions. Bonne journée!

Thanks for writing. Your order shipped on [date] and should arrive by [date]. Here's your tracking link.

2. I want to return an item

Bonjour [Prénom],

Merci de votre message. Nous sommes désolés que l'article ne vous convienne pas. Vous pouvez nous le retourner d'ici le [date] en suivant ces étapes : [instructions].

Dès que nous le recevrons, nous traiterons votre remboursement dans un délai de [X] jours ouvrables.

Sorry the item didn't work out. Return it by [date] using these steps; we'll refund you within [X] business days of receiving it.

3. Your order is delayed

Bonjour [Prénom],

Nous tenons à vous informer que votre commande n° [numéro] a pris du retard. Elle devrait maintenant être expédiée le [date].

Nous sommes désolés pour ce contretemps et vous remercions de votre patience.

Your order is delayed and should now ship on [date]. Sorry, and thank you for your patience.

4. Your refund is on its way

Bonjour [Prénom],

Votre remboursement de [montant] $ a été effectué aujourd'hui. Selon votre institution financière, il pourrait prendre de 5 à 10 jours ouvrables avant d'apparaître sur votre relevé.

Merci de votre patience!

Your refund of $[amount] was issued today. It can take 5 to 10 business days to show on your statement.

5. Thanks for your order (and a review request)

Bonjour [Prénom],

Merci d'avoir magasiné chez nous! Si vous avez un moment, votre avis nous aiderait beaucoup : [lien].

Au plaisir de vous servir à nouveau!

Thanks for shopping with us! If you have a moment, a review would help us a lot.

Four details that make French replies feel right

  • Reply in the language the customer used. If they wrote in French, answer in French.
  • Write money the Canadian French way: 25,00 $, with a comma and the dollar sign after the number.
  • Use Canadian words: courriel (email), magasiner (to shop), fin de semaine (weekend). See our list of 15 words.
  • Keep your signature bilingual if your brand is bilingual, but don't mix languages inside the message itself.
